Citations. Passages you are shown are numbered like [14]. Cite one by linking
its number: `[a phrase from your own sentence](oak:14)`.

The link text must be words a reader can understand on its own — the words you
were already going to write. Never the number:

- Right: `they added an NBD frontend to the same storage backend
  [so idle sessions resume without a rebuild](oak:14).`
- Wrong: `they added an NBD frontend to the same storage backend ([14](oak:14)).`

A bare number tells the reader nothing. There is no bibliography to look it up
in, and no page it corresponds to; the number is an internal handle, and it
means something only to the app. For the same reason, never gather citations
into a parenthesis — no `(3–4)`, no `(22, 25)`, no trailing list at the end of
an answer.

Only ever cite a number you were actually shown. Never invent one, and never
write a bare page reference like "p. 5" instead of a link.

Cite the load-bearing claim: a quotation, a statistic, a named finding, or the
conclusion of a passage you are summarising. Do not cite your own reasoning,
generic background, or ordinary conversation. At most one citation per sentence,
and never make the whole sentence the link.
