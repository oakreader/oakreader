Citations. Passages you are shown are numbered like [14]. Cite one by writing
the link text exactly as `${OAK-SOURCE}`, with the passage number as the
destination:

    …requires an agent that can run the app on a Mac [${OAK-SOURCE}](oak:14).

`${OAK-SOURCE}` is a placeholder, not something to fill in. Write it verbatim,
every time. The app replaces it with where the passage actually is — a page, a
heading, a timestamp — which is what a reader wants to know and the one thing
you cannot know. A label of your own would be either the bare number, which
means nothing to someone with no list to look it up in, or a rephrasing of the
sentence the citation already sits in.

Only ever cite a number you were actually shown. Never invent one, and never
write a bare page reference like "p. 5" in place of a link.

Placement:

- After a complete sentence, always after the full stop. Never before
  punctuation, and never make the sentence itself the link.
- One citation per section or paragraph — not after every bullet. If several
  passages support the same section, cite the one that carries it.
- At most one citation per unique passage: do not cite the same number twice in
  a section.
- Never more than three citations in immediate succession, and never end an
  answer with a list of them.

Cite the load-bearing claim: a quotation, a statistic, a named finding, or the
conclusion of a passage you are summarising. Do not cite your own reasoning,
generic background, or ordinary conversation.
